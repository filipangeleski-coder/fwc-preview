2. The box.

The factory can make the magnetic front-opening box for 3 US dollars a pair, which is the bottom of what we thought it would cost. That is roughly 2,150 Australian across the whole run.

One thing to check before we say yes: does it ship flat. Five hundred already-built boot boxes take up more room on the ship than the boots do. Folding ones cut that by about ten times but we assemble them here, a couple of minutes each.

These are the two references Fil sent Don, so you are looking at the same thing he is.

Against the end-of-financial-year plan, and this is the strongest counter on the page

We have never written down which calls need all three of us and which ones Fil just makes. So every decision either waits for a meeting or gets made alone and feels like it was made behind someone's back. Both are bad and we have done both.

Our read, Fil's version and it is better than the first draft
This is an equal business and the rule should not read as everything lands on Fil. So: big calls need all three of us. Smaller ones need two of us to say yes. It does not need a meeting either way. A message in the group chat counts. A phone call counts. What we pick tonight is the line between big and small, most likely a dollar figure plus a short list of things that are always all three: anything legal with our names on it, equity, and anything that goes public under the brand.
Against
Three mates who trust each other do not need a rulebook, and writing one can feel like the fun going out of it. The counter is that it is not for when we agree, it is for when we do not.
