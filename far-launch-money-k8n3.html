Read that bottom row again
If we hand Dave the full $66,000 at the end of run one, we cannot afford a second run even if we sell out completely. We would be $18,212 short. FAR would have sold every boot it owns, paid everyone, and then stopped.

If we pay Dave his $6,000 interest and keep the $60,000 working, we can order run two from 40% sell-through onward, which is 181 pairs.

What the loan paperwork actually says

We have now read the agreement Dave's side is using. The dollar figures in it are still blanks, but the rules are not, and they are better for us than we assumed.

  • Paying interest and holding onto the money is allowed. It is written into the document. Word for word: the principal is repayable in such amounts as the borrower decides from time to time, as long as the whole lot is repaid by the end of the term. We choose when we pay the principal down. That is not a favour we have to ask for.
  • The term in the form is two years, not one. Two years gives us run one and run two to pay it back, instead of one run.
  • Interest is once a year, on the anniversary. Not monthly. One payment, once a year.
Two things to nail down with Dave before anything is signed
Is the 10% per year, or 10% once? The document reads like 10% every year, which over two years is $12,000 rather than $6,000. That is a real six thousand dollar difference and it is one question.

The interest payment cannot be missed. If it is not paid on the anniversary, the document adds it to the loan AND makes the whole thing repayable on demand. One missed payment turns a two year loan into a pay-it-now loan. We will have about $41,000 on hand at that point so it is affordable, but it goes in the calendar the day the loan starts.

The real problem is timing, not the monthly cost
About $49,000 leaves our hands before a single boot is sold, and it leaves in one hit. The factory wants the full balance before anything gets on a ship. Then the stock is at sea for four to six weeks. Then it sells over about five months. The gap between paying the factory and the money coming back is the thing that kills small importers. Not the $749 a month.

Promises we have made that nobody had costed

  • The TIACS donation. $5 from every boot to the tradie mental health mob. That is $2,250 on this run, and it is in the numbers above now. We need to actually talk to them before we say it publicly.
  • The referral reward has no limit on it. Ten referrals gets someone a free pair, and that is live on the website right now with no cap. Every one of those costs us about $100.
  • $35,000 of stock sitting in three houses with no insurance on it. Home insurance usually does not cover business stock. None of us has checked our own policy.
  • Product insurance is quoted, not bought. $2,080 a year. It has to be paid before the first boot ships, and before Grant and Lionel wear the samples on site.
