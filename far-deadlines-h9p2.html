Written 22 September. Not a to-do list. This is only the stuff where a date passes and something real breaks. Everything on here has a consequence written next to it. If a job has no consequence, it's not on this page.

30 September

Switch our GST reporting to monthly

FilAccountant

To not pay GST in cash at the border, the tax office wants us registered for GST (we are, since June) and lodging BAS monthly. We currently lodge quarterly, and a switch only takes effect from the next quarter.

What breaks
Miss 30 September and the next window is 1 January, which is after the boots land. We'd hand over roughly $2,200 to $2,400 in cash at the dock instead of deferring it. It's a form. It's the cheapest thing on this page and it has the tightest date.
Before the bags get sewn

Tell the bag factory each bag needs a label on it

Fil

We paid the bag deposit on 22 September and they're a 20 to 25 day run, so they're being made right now. Australian law says every bag has to carry "Made in China" plus a true description of what it is, on the bag itself. Nobody has ever told Deby this. Same goes for the socks before we place that order.

What breaks
Goods that arrive without that label can be seized and forfeited to the Crown. The bags and socks travel inside the boot boxes on one shipment, so one unlabelled item can hold up the whole lot. This is a one-line email and it has to go before they're made, not after.

This week

Decide the slide mould, then order

Fil Grant Lionel

We decided the slides go in the first 100 to 150 boxes. They have the longest lead time of anything we're buying: 45 to 60 days to make, plus 25 to 40 days on the water, and they ship out of a different port so they can't travel with the boots. Boots now land about the first week of January. The factory now says an order around 8 October finishes at the end of January and ships around 10 February, in the middle of the Chinese New Year holiday. That's double the 45 to 60 days they gave us in writing on 10 September, so we've pushed back and asked for the first 150 pairs by early December, so we can fly them in. Their answer lands around 8 October.

The factory's sample arrived on 30 September. It's a size 41, so none of us can wear it, and the sole feels harder and less premium than the August slides. The samples with our logo in our sizes land around 9 October. The three of us decide at the meeting and once the factory answers.

The call
Sizes 47 and 48 don't exist in the original August slide. New moulds cost US$2,000 (about $2,900 AUD) and another 30 days. The other way is the new mould, if the factory can make it in the softer August material. We're asking that before anyone pays for moulds.

Two more things: we can choose our own quantity per size (the factory confirmed), and customs rules say every slide needs what it's made of and "Made in China" pressed into it. Plenty of brands just use a sticker, so we've asked our customs broker whether a sticker would pass, and asked the factory what carving it would cost (they carve our logo for free).

Decide where the stock actually goes

FilGrant

Storage unit at Kennards Wollongong (about $224 or $397 a month depending on the unit) versus Fil's house. Fil and Lionel both voted "Fil's house, but only if his insurer puts a business-stock endorsement in writing first." Grant hasn't voted.

What breaks

The house doesn't cover $29,000 of stock without that written endorsement, and picking it up ourselves in the borrowed truck turns an insured trip into an uninsured one.

Actually bind the insurance

Fil

We have a quote for about $2,000. It is a quote, not a policy. The broker pack is written and has never been sent. Two separate covers: the business policy and the transit cover.

What breaks
Because we import the boot and put our name on it, the law treats FAR as the manufacturer. The policy has to cover PRODUCT liability, not just public liability. Unbound product cover at launch is the biggest uninsured hole in the business.

Why this page exists

None of our documents carried a due date. The job list knows every job. The shipping plan knows every owner. Neither of them knows what day anything is due, so a job could sit past the last day it could have been done and nothing anywhere would say a word.

The GST one is the example. It was written down on 14 September with "30 September" attached to it, and eight days out nothing had ever pushed it at anyone. That's being fixed properly on the dashboard. This page is the version you can read on your phone in the meantime.
