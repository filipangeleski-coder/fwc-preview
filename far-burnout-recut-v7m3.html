The Burnout, re-cut

Fil's 27 July video of the car going over the dead boots. It felt plain, so it's been re-edited with moving captions and tighter shots, and tested with AI on the opening. Three versions below. Only the first three seconds differ.

Answer the three questions

Which opening, post it or re-shoot, and which last line. There's a box under each one for anything else. Answers by Fri 2 Oct.

The three versions

1. Real footage recommended

Every frame is what the phone shot. Colour pulled back, sky darkened, captions on.

2. AI dust AI edit

The real crush with a puff of dust and grit added where the tyre meets the boot. Everything else stays real. The catch: a car rolling over a boot on clean tarmac doesn't really throw dust, and a tradie might notice.

3. AI storm light AI edit

The real crush with the sunny sky turned to grey storm cloud and flat light. The moodiest of the three. The catch: the next shot, Fil walking in, is blue sky, so the weather changes in one cut. Fil's own shots can't be changed the same way, because his face never goes into these AI tools.

What changed from the plain version

  • Opens on the tyre already on the boot, slowed down, with "A BAD BOOT ISN'T AN ACCIDENT" on screen from the first frame.
  • Fil's best take of each line, in order: "That's a thousand bucks down the drain." Then "I always thought I was just paying for the name" over the crushed pile. Then "And now I know that I was right." Then "Someone chose this for you." Then "Some bloke that's never been to a site in his life." It ends on "The manufacturer is not the problem. It's what you decide to put in the boot." said while he's sitting in the car's boot holding one.
  • Every line as a caption, the key word flipped to black on white.
  • Tighter framing on the car-boot shots, so the number plate never shows and the sky mostly goes.
  • Colour pulled back, sky darkened, fine grain. No music: this one is Fil talking straight.
  • The model tab and markings on the dead boots are blurred, so no brand can be picked out.

The honest part

What made it plain was mostly where it was shot: a sunny carpark and a small car rolling gently over boots that barely squash. Captions and tight shots help the talking half a lot. They can't make the crush heavier than it was, and the AI can only dress the crush shot, not Fil's shots.

If the opening still feels light, the fix is a 20-minute re-shoot of just the crush: dirt or gravel instead of a carpark, the deadest pairs we've got, phone low and behind the tyre, no sky, no plate. Everything Fil says to camera stays.
