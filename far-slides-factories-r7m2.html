PVC or EVA?

EVA sole, PU strap. Every premium slide we checked uses EVA-type foam, and none uses PVC. A PU strap with a raised logo on an EVA sole is the Adilette build, and it's what HEVA was making.

The catch with EVA is heat. A car parked in Brisbane summer sun reached 67°C in 15 minutes, and ordinary EVA starts to shrink or warp from about 70°C. Whoever we pick has to show the slide survives a 70°C test, or a slide left in the ute comes back a size smaller.

Did HEVA waste our time?

Partly. But size wasn't really the problem. On 16 Sep HEVA offered a second sole that already went to 47/48, and we turned it down because it looked cheap. What actually killed HEVA is the date: end of January, shipping into Chinese New Year.

HEVA also isn't small: it claims factories in four countries. "Fully booked" is peak season, and every factory is in it now. That's the real risk with a new one too. Their branded sample pairs ship this week, so keep them as the quality benchmark.

The customs mark

Australian law says MADE IN CHINA and the upper and sole materials must be pressed or moulded into the slide, letters at least 2.5mm high. If the material won't take that, a pressed or moulded rubber, plastic or cloth label goes on instead. HEVA's printed clear sticker meets neither. Every factory gets asked this up front.
