What a switch video is

Start with any stranger's viral clip where something's about to go wrong: a trip, a stack, a fail. At the exact moment it goes wrong, cut to one of us doing the same move. About 10 seconds, no talking. People stay because they want to see the crash, and then it's us.

The clip can be anything, and so can our half. The only thing that has to be right is the cut: it lands on the same movement, so it feels like one video, not two. There are thousands of these clips out there. Every one is a new video.

Watch how others do it

  • Same moment: a stranger dives onto an air mattress, and it cuts to the singer Alex Warren already mid-fall, holding his new album. 10.3 million views. Watch
  • Same sound: a cow kicks a farmhand, and it cuts on the hit to Alex Warren falling. 4.1 million. Watch
  • The aftermath: a crash on the street, then a cut to him in bed, bandaged up with his album. 1.7 million. Watch
  • A clothing brand, twice: Wants and Needs did one and got 14 million, then did it again with a different clip and got 9 million. Same recipe, new clip. Watch
  • Re-film it yourself: the same brand copying famous viral moments with their own gear in them. Watch

Find your own: scroll fails and stacks on Instagram or TikTok. Anything where it goes wrong in the first two seconds. Put the link on the Ideas board so the rest of us can see it.

Rules for our half: if someone falls, it's in old boots, never the FARs. Soft landing, walking pace, nothing higher than your knee. Backyard, driveway or porch, never a work site or a footpath. No house numbers, street signs or plates in shot. Phone upright, three takes, keep rolling 5 seconds after.
