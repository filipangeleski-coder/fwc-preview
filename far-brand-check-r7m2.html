Why we did this

An automatic email reply to Xavier promised him a "wide fit option". We only make one boot. It's a bit wider than the narrow boots, but there's no wide option. The helper got it from our own FAQ and DM notes, which were out of date. So we checked everything the email and DM helpers read, all the brand docs, the website, and the helpers' own instructions.

What we found

About 500 out-of-date lines across nearly 100 files. The ones that mattered most:

  • Sizes. The FAQ said AU 6 to 13. The real run is nine sizes, AU 7 to 14.
  • The sole. The FAQ and press kit said "nitrile, rated to 300 degrees". It's rubber, with no heat rating we can back.
  • The leather. We were saying "full leather". It's nubuck, with a fabric panel by the zip.
  • The safety standard. We were writing "AS/NZS 2210.3". The certificate is AS 2210.3:2019, held by our factory.
  • Who we are. The press kit still said "three mates" and named Safetoe as our maker. It's the four of us, and Estar makes the boots.
  • The offer. Old $229 prices, "Founding 500", referral rewards and MATE20 were still being offered. All fixed to $219 for the first 100, then $249. MATE20 is parked (Fil, 10 Oct).
  • The helpers themselves. The supplier helper still thought Safetoe was our boot maker. It now knows it's Don at Estar, and never to mention another factory to him. Four other helpers had numbers from June (471 followers). Fixed and live.

Two things are still on the website, and Fil publishes those: the sign-up message says "first pick when the 500 open" (should be the first 100), and the delivery line is a call already waiting on Fil.

New: a weekly brand watch

Every Sunday at 5pm the system reads new posts from six Instagram accounts and ten podcasts and newsletters about brands, marketing and psychology. It checks each idea against what we already do, throws out anything that breaks our rules, and leaves the best few for the next session to judge. Nobody has to send links for it to work.

It reads captions and view counts through Instagram's own business tools (allowed). It doesn't download videos (that's call C3 on the poll).

The two accounts Fil sent

Yasmin (cult-brand psychology) is good for naming why things work. Almost everything she teaches, we already do under other names. Two of the effects she uses have failed when scientists re-tested them, so we don't build on those.

Carson (marketing stunts) is good for cheap physical ideas. Most of his 30 formats we already have, or they're illegal in NSW, or too expensive. Four were new for us (below).

Both run their accounts to sell their agency work. Follow them from your own account if you want them in your feed, not from FAR's. Our following list is public. The weekly watch covers them either way.

Six ideas

1. Five-second page test

ConFilCon shows 10 tradies in the chair our ask message and the private page for five seconds: what is this, who's it for? Under 8 of 10 = rewrite before it opens. Free.

2. Sticker test before we print

GrantFilGrant wears his top 3 designs on his hat for a week; 10 tradies we know say if they'd stick one on theirs. Print only what 7 of 10 would. Free, saves money.

3. "Things blokes say in my chair"

ConA list reel of real lines Con hears, and the joke is he slips and repeats one. No "send this to your mate". Free.

4. Sticker-and-card swap

ConSwap stickers and cards with 3 to 5 small tradie brands that don't sell boots. No money either way. Needs call C2.

5. Pub coasters

GrantLionelCoasters with our belief line at the pubs you drink at, publican's yes first. About $75 to $160. After the first 100.

6. Tear-off tabs

GrantOn the smoko-room posters, so a bloke can take the web address without scanning in front of the crew. Free.
