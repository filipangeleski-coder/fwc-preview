Opening the pre-sale

What has to be true before we can take a single dollar, what happens on the day, and who owns each line. Written 27 Aug. Read the bit with your name on it.

The honest number, so nobody reads a good week as a bad one

Week one: expect four to six deposits. Anywhere from one to fourteen and we are inside normal.

That is not pessimism, it is arithmetic on what we have actually done before. Our email list is around 118 people. The last three times we emailed all of them we got three clicks in total and nobody bought anything, because there was nothing to buy. Our own first outreach wave got 8 out of 50 people to respond, and that was asking them for nothing. Asking for twenty bucks on a boot with no delivery date is harder.

What this means
Fifty deposits is an eight to twelve week job, not a launch-day number. Agree that BEFORE the week starts, or a real six gets read as a failure against an imaginary fifty.

Two things nobody can settle except the three of us

The meeting

Saturday's meeting booked a follow-up for "next week". Nobody has recorded whether it happened. It holds four calls and nothing moves until it does: which Monday, the freebies, who fronts the launch episode, and whether deposit-holders get the price locked. The numbers for all four are on the pre-read page.

The lawyer line

On 27 July all three of us voted, three to nil, that a lawyer reads the terms before the first dollar. On Saturday that got parked in the room. Parked is not the same as overturned. Turning the product on now goes against a decision all three of us made.

There is also no money for it right now. So it is a real call, not a formality: re-vote it, or find a cheaper read. Either way it gets said out loud and written down, not drifted past.

Fifteen minutes each. Fil.

  • Check three settings in the shop: is a card processor actually turned on, is GST set up, is anything pasted in the checkout scripts box.
  • Kill the fake counter. The site says "92 tradies in". It is typed in by hand, nothing updates it, and we voted to keep the number private until fifty.
  • Rewrite the Instagram bio. It currently says "4 Mates building the fix" and "Australia's comfiest work boot". There are three of us, and we cannot prove comfiest on a boot nobody has worn yet.
  • Archive eight email automations sitting live in Klaviyo that none of us set up. Harmless now. The day someone pays, they can email a customer something none of us wrote.
  • Generate three tracked links, one per person, so we can see whose messages actually worked.

Anyone who pays gets a message from a human within the hour. Not the automatic receipt. His name, his size, roughly where he sits in the queue, and a promise that he hears about the factory order before anyone else. Ninety seconds each. Whoever is free does it.

When we call it

Agreed before we start, so nobody has to argue about it later.

  • Read the week, on Sunday. Never a single night. Deposits and follows lag days behind the thing that caused them.
  • Under three deposits with everyone's texts actually sent: the offer is wrong, not the effort. Fix the offer before burning another week of content on it.
  • Nobody buys but people are clicking through: the page is the problem, not the demand. We missed exactly this once before and it took 24 days to notice.
  • Eight weeks, under fifteen deposits: the deposit has failed at its job, which was to tell us what sizes to order. We size the factory run off the free list and Grant's and Lionel's read of the trade instead, and decide together whether to leave the deposit open.

Not doing, and why

  • No ads until at least one person we did not chase puts money down.
  • No countdown timer or fake deadline. The deposit exists to tell us what sizes to make. Closing it early just means less information.
  • No public counter until fifty. A low number on screen does more harm than no number.
  • No Afterpay. We probably do not qualify, and their rules put pre-orders on the banned list anyway. Four payments of five dollars on a refundable twenty is pointless.
  • No delivery date. But we do not go quiet either. We say why there is no date, give an honest window, say it might move, and then actually keep people posted. Being straight about the fine print is the one thing our competitors structurally cannot copy.
  • No ads on the comedy posts. Ever. That is what the bio and the pinned comment are for.

The one that is not about launch day

Right now the three of us are a partnership in the eyes of the law. That means if anything goes wrong, the money comes out of all three of us personally, without limit. Same for the Dave Santos loan.

Equal thirds, nothing signed, no rule about what needs a three-way vote and what one of us can just decide. Setting up a company is cheap. Not setting one up while taking money from the public should be a decision we actually make, not something we drift into.

And the insurance question sits under all of it. We are about to sell safety footwear with a puncture plate in it, and there is no cover written down anywhere.

Worth saying plainly
The argument is never really about the socks. Have this conversation before we take money off customers, not after.
