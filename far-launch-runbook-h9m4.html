The lawyer, and the money we do not have

On 27 July all three of us voted, three to nil, that a lawyer reads the terms before we take a dollar. On Saturday that got parked in the room. Parked is not overturned. Turning the product on now goes directly against a decision all three of us made.

And there is no money for it. The cheapest proper fixed-fee read we found is $499 plus GST. We do not have it right now.

So this is a real fork, not a formality:

  • Re-vote it and open without a lawyer, knowing what that means.
  • Or find the $499 and wait two to three business days.
  • Or find a cheaper read. There are free and low-cost options for small business and none of them have been checked yet.

Whichever way it goes, it gets said out loud and written down. Drifting past a unanimous vote because it got awkward is how three people end up remembering three different agreements.

And the four months nobody has planned for

Deposits open around September. Boots land December to February at the earliest. There is no plan anywhere for stopping someone asking for their twenty bucks back across a four-month wait with a date that might move.

The research is blunt on this: roughly seven in ten people forgive a delay if you give them a reason and an honest estimate. Roughly three in ten forgive it if you go quiet.

So we write down an update schedule before we take the first dollar, and then we actually keep it. That is the cheapest thing protecting our money, and it costs nothing but not forgetting.

When we call it dead

Agreed now, so nobody has to argue about it while feeling defensive later.

  • Read the week, on Sunday. Never a single night. Deposits and follows lag days behind whatever caused them.
  • Under three deposits with everyone's texts actually sent and logged: the offer is wrong, not the effort. Fix the offer before burning another week of content on it.
  • Nobody buying but people clicking: the page is the problem.
  • Eight weeks, under fifteen deposits: the deposit has failed at its job. We size the factory order off the free list and Grant's and Lionel's read of the trade instead, and decide together whether to leave it open. Written down now with a named person who calls it, or it becomes a rolling extension nobody ever ends.

What we are not doing, and why

  • No ads until at least one person we did not personally chase puts money down.
  • No referral rewards or waitlist leaderboards. Every number supporting them comes from companies selling the software. On a base of ten deposits it is under one extra sale for a weekend of building. And there is a live problem here: our FAQ currently promises free socks for three referrals, and every order already ships with free socks. The first bloke who does that maths in public does it to a brand whose whole thing is not shafting tradies. Fix or pull it.
  • No Afterpay. We probably do not qualify with no trading history, and their own merchant rules list pre-orders as prohibited. Four payments of five dollars on a refundable twenty is pointless anyway.
  • No countdown or hard close date. The deposit exists to tell us what to make. Closing early just means less information.
  • No public counter until fifty. A low number on screen actively hurts. What is missing is deciding NOW, in writing, what it says at fifty and who flips it, so it is not an argument at the moment it should be firing.
  • No website rebuild. The number that justified one has been retracted. Fix the four specific faults and stop.
  • No size curve as content. Ever.
  • No ask on the comedy posts. Ever.
  • No delivery date. And no going quiet either. We say why there is no date, give an honest window, say it might move, and keep people posted. Being straight about the fine print is the one thing our competitors structurally cannot copy, because theirs is written by lawyers to hide things.

The three that decide how bad this can get

1. The company protects you. Two things still don't.

Corrected 28 Aug. An earlier version of this page said we were an unincorporated partnership and that every debt came out of all three of us personally. That was wrong. We are FAR WORKWEAR PTY LTD, ABN 65 699 642 030, registered 29 June.

That's the strongest protection you two have. If this goes under, the debt belongs to the company, not to your house. Say that plainly because it's true and it matters.

But two things go straight through a company.

  • The loan probably has a personal guarantor, and nobody has read the actual contract. Corrected 29 Aug, and this is the important correction on the page. The earlier version said flatly that Dave's loan has a guarantor and that "whoever signed carries it personally". That was describing the blank Redchip template on Fil's Drive, not the signed Dave Santos agreement. The template has Borrower = the Pty Ltd, Lender = a self-managed super fund, and a named individual as Guarantor, with a survival clause that still bites if the guarantee itself fails. What we actually know about the real contract is that it exists, it is written, the company is the borrower, and 10% interest goes to Dave's super inside the first year. Nobody at FAR has read the guarantee clause. So this is not "someone is on the hook", it is "we do not know whether anyone is on the hook, and that is worse". Job 9 on this page answers it and it is a one-hour job. Two things to know before that conversation: a company cannot guarantee its own debt, so FAR WORKWEAR PTY LTD is not an option for the guarantor line, and "all three of us sign" is not the safe middle, because a guarantee is joint and several and any one signer can be pursued for the whole amount, not a third of it.
  • Directors' duties are personal. The big one is insolvent trading: keep taking people's money knowing we can't deliver, and that lands on us as individuals. No company stops that. It is exactly why the delivery-date discipline and the terms page are not paperwork.

2. Insurance is quoted, not bought

Updated 28 Aug. Troy has quoted us about $2,000 and Fil has it. That is real progress on what used to be a total blank. But a quote is not cover. Nothing protects us until it is paid and there is a certificate.

Two things still have to be checked on that quote before anyone treats this as closed. Does it cover PRODUCT liability or only public? Product is the one that matters for a boot with a plate in it, and a public-only policy would look sorted while leaving the actual risk wide open. And does it cover boots sold before the policy starts, because deposits may land first.

This is still the one item on the page that is genuinely terminal if it goes wrong. It just moved from nothing to nearly.

3. There is a registered FAR trade mark in our exact class

Checked directly on IP Australia 28 Aug. Number 2204535, the word FAR, filed 2021, registered 2022, Class 25 which is clothing, footwear and headgear. That is precisely our class and precisely our product.

Two things stop this being a death sentence. It is registered as a stylised mark rather than the plain word, so the protection attaches to a particular look and ours is different. And the register does not display the owner, so we do not yet know who holds it or whether they are even trading.

What does not automatically fix it is calling ourselves FAR Workwear, because a word that just describes what you sell carries little weight when the two names are compared. This is a lawyer question, not a forum question. A pre-sale is the loudest and most screenshotted thing FAR has ever done under this name.

Book the IP conversation in the same call as the terms read.
