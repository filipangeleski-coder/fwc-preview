The boot decided

What we sellOne boot. The zip-up. The slip-on comes later, once the zip is selling.
Who makes itEstar in Qingdao. Don is our bloke there. Fil and Lionel walked the factory in July.
The soleRubber rated to 300 degrees. Most boots run a plastic sole that gives up at 130 to 140. Estar sent the test report, so it is real, not a sales line.
UnderfootPU foam midsole. Memory foam insole with a Poron pad in the heel.
The plate4mm Kevlar between the insole and midsole so a nail cannot come up through your foot. Non-metallic, not steel.
The restSteel toe cap, nubuck leather, mesh lining, zip plus laces. Ankle padding went from 12mm to 15mm after the samples felt thin.
Safety certCertified to the Australian standard, held by Estar, runs to 2031. Designed here, built in China to the Aussie standard. We never say Australian made.

Two things we used to say that are now dead: the Adidas-style foam midsole (Estar will not run it, it wrecks on site) and anything about the plate being lighter than steel (never tested on this boot, so we do not claim it).

What went wrong with the samples

  • They came back a size small. Not the factory's fault. They were ordered in UK sizes and Estar's UK sizing runs a size below what we call the same number here. Grant's pair fits because his accidentally landed right.
  • There was a raised lip inside where the toe cap meets the sole. The lining was folded. Don is fixing it on the samples and on the whole run.
  • Fixed for good: every order goes to the factory in millimetres now, not size numbers. The tag in the boot and on the box prints the Aussie size, big and first.

When it happens no date, on purpose

There is no launch date and anyone who gives you one is guessing.

It is a chain. We sign the big factory order, and roughly three to four months later boots are in our hands. That is when launch week starts.

Where we are in that chain right now: two test pairs in the bigger sizes are paid for and being built with the thicker ankle padding. Nobody asked Don for a ship date, so nothing is late. Best guess two to three weeks. When they land, all three of us wear a correctly sized pair and give a verdict. That verdict unlocks the size range, which unlocks the 500-pair order, which starts the clock.

The old rule that we needed 500 emails before launching is dead. Fil overruled it. Tradies buy boots, they do not join mailing lists.

Price only ever goes up, never down. No sales, no Black Friday, no haggling in the DMs. Being early is the discount.

$249 is what the big Aussie boot brands charge. We are not the cheap option, we are the built-different option.

One discount code will ever exist: MATE20. Hand a mate a referral card, he gets $20 off and so do you. Afterpay at the checkout, four payments.

What is in the box

Every order: boots, FAR socks, a canvas boot bag, a Day 1 card, a feedback card with a QR code, three to five stickers, free postage anywhere in Australia.

First 100 orders also get a handwritten note from Fil, a numbered founding card, and his actual mobile number. That part never gets automated or faked.

The number goes on the card, not stamped into the boot. Lionel worked out why: if someone with a refundable deposit pulls out, a numbered size 9 is stuck with nobody able to buy it.

The $20 deposit

All three of you voted this in, 3 to 0, all six questions, no notes. Twenty bucks holds a spot in the line for the first 500. Refundable any time, no questions, back on the card within 5 working days. At launch it comes off the boot automatically.

It is not buying a boot. No delivery date promised, no promise your size is still there, and you cannot hand your spot to a mate.

The page is built and sitting switched off. It stays off until the boots pass our own wear test and until the lawyer and accountant come back.

Be realistic about what it tells us: at our current traffic that is tens of deposits, not hundreds. It answers "will anyone actually pay", not "how many size 10s do we order".

The promise we make buyers not settled

This is the one thing on this page we cannot currently state properly, and it needs sorting before anything goes on the website.

There are three different warranty stories written across our own documents right now. One says 90 days. One says 18 months on defects plus 30 days comfort. One says 30 days. They cannot all be true and a customer will read whichever one we publish.

On top of that, we currently promise the boot is water resistant for 12 months and we have never received a test report backing it. The question is drafted to Don and has not been sent.

The intended promise, once it is agreed: first 500 get 18 months on anything that breaks because we built it wrong, 30 days to wear them on site and send them back if they do not feel right, and 12 months on the water resistance. Send a photo and your order number, we decide inside 5 working days, we pay the return postage every time.

How someone actually finds us and buys mostly decided

Four tools do the work. KickoffLabs catches the email and hands out referral links. Klaviyo sends the emails. Shopify is the website and will be the checkout. The last one just points the web address at the right place.

What works today: someone watches a reel, taps the bio link, lands on the site, puts an email in, and gets one welcome email. That is the only automated email that has ever properly worked.

What just got built: six more emails, one a week, warming people up before launch. Written back in March, never wired up, finally built as a draft on 15 August. Sitting switched off waiting for Fil to read it and flip it live.

What has never worked: the referral rewards. Three mates gets socks, five gets slides, ten gets a free pair. In a year it has sent three emails, got zero opens, and the top tier has never once fired. The two blokes who did earn something were sorted by hand.

Two things worth knowing

  • The funnel broke silently for three and a half weeks in July. People signed up, saw "you're in", and never landed in the email system. Nobody noticed for 25 days. There is now a watchdog that checks every morning and names anyone missing.
  • Because FAR is a common word, every call to action says "search FAR Workwear", never "search FAR". That is how we own the search people will actually type.

If it is quiet

There is a rule so nobody panics. Under 20 orders in two days means something is broken and we go find out which part. 20 to 50 orders is not a flop, it is real money, and we post the number honestly.

The physical work needs bodies

The week before launch there is real footwork, and right now the plan has Fil doing all of it on his own:

  • Posters up in smoko rooms
  • Stickers and cards dropped at trade suppliers
  • A TAFE drop
  • Pubs on Friday arvo
  • Standing at a site gate at 6:30am handing out stickers the day before launch

That is about 10 to 12 hours across the week, mostly early mornings. The gear itself is cheap, roughly a grand for 5,000 stickers, 100 posters, 500 counter cards, 200 stubby holders and 500 referral cards.

This is the most obvious thing to share out. It needs people who are already on sites at 6:30am, which is you two, not him.

Free pairs

Nobody gets a free pair before launch. Not one. Seeding only starts after the store is live and any day-one dramas are sorted.

Then 40 boots go out, split two ways: 20 to Aussie tradie creators, 20 handed to site foremen in person at the gate between 6:30 and 7am, by one of us, not a courier.

You never ask a creator to post. Send the boots with no strings and shut up. If they are good, he posts because he wants to.

The part that never stops

The day boots ship, a whole new job starts and it does not go away: orders, sizing questions, wrong sizes coming back, warranty claims, reviews. Every reply has a clock on it. Answer within a day. Decide a warranty claim inside 5 working days. Return label within 2. Replacement out within 10.

Straight answer on this one: that is not a founder job, it is a hire. None of us can absorb it on top of everything else. Worth knowing now so it does not land as a surprise.

Straight talk: what you two are carrying

You are equal owners, not talent. That means some of this is yours whether anyone has said it out loud or not. None of it is a reason to stop. All of it is a reason to sort the paperwork.

1. There is no insurance yet

No product liability, no public liability. For a safety boot sold to blokes working at height, the going benchmark for this kind of supplier is $20 million product and $10 million public cover. We have none of it and the budget still has insurance sitting at zero.

Until that is bought and the company structure is properly finished, a serious injury claim does not stop at the business.

2. Nothing is signed between us

Equal thirds was agreed verbally in June. There is no signed agreement, no effective date on paper, and the money already does not reconcile: the loans sheet has one of us in for $1,430 and two at $1,275, while the budget models everyone at $733.

Nothing written covers what happens if one of us wants out, stops filming, or if a decision splits two against one.

3. The name may not be ours to use

There is a trademark already registered to someone else in our clothing category. That is verified, not a rumour. The lawyer consult on it is still pending.

If it ever forced a rebrand, every video you have filmed goes with it. You are entitled to know that before putting another year in.

4. The honest downside

If we order stock and sell nothing, the money at risk is between $27,045 and $45,075. We need to sell about 293 of the 500 pairs just to get the stock money back. Nobody is drawing a wage.

The line of credit that covers the gap is still only in progress, and the factory payment terms we have assumed have never actually been quoted by Don.

5. Your face and your job

Written permission for using your likeness has never been signed. It needs to be, and it protects you as much as the business.

Grant's employer objection is still open four months later. It has already killed three episodes, and one episode sitting at 91,823 views could still be pulled. That is Grant's actual job on the table and it needs a written answer, not a verbal one.

On site: filming somewhere we have not cleared kills the footage. It has already happened once and two finished bits went in the bin.
