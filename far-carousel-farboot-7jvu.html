Your idea: drag them in, then cut to you falling off a scooter

Every link you sent was watched end to end (Fil's TikTok is the exception: TikTok has it locked as "sensitive content", so I watched three other posts from that account instead). Then I checked what FAR has already learned, what Instagram and copyright law say, and drafted 15 versions.

How the Alex Warren ones work

His three album reels did 10.4M, 4.1M and 1.7M plays. Each opens on about one to two seconds of someone else's fail clip (a bloke diving onto an air mattress, a cow kicking a handler, a bull), then a hard cut to him already mid-fall on the same action. The cut lands at 1.0 to 1.7 seconds. The whole thing runs 7 to 12 seconds. The plug is the punchline: he gets up holding the record, and the cover is him falling in the same outfit. The biggest one has no words and no ask at all.

Caveat: he's famous and it was album week. Plays tell you reach, not whether anyone bought, and the three dropped from 10.4M to 1.7M in a week as the gag wore thin.

We've done this before

The Burnout. You asked for a viral clip first ("the whole point is having that viral clip first"), it got built with the ball-squash clip, and you killed it: "the ball squish is ridiculous". What we learned then is the same thing Warren does: the open has to be the same action as our first shot, cut inside two seconds, so it's one joke and not two videos. Our Grease open waited 11 seconds to show us and held 14%, the worst we've had.

The rights, in two lines

Instagram won't punish one borrowed clip inside an otherwise original reel. Its originality rule is about accounts that mostly repost. Copyright is the real problem: Australian law has no exception for using someone's clip to advertise, so a valid report means the post comes down within about a day, and repeat strikes can lose the account. This is the same account that already lost the Old Fella.

The clean way is what the clothing brand in the McKeaveney reel does: re-film the viral moment ourselves, then cut. People still recognise the moment, and none of the footage is anyone else's.

The three I'd build

1. Stunt Double (my pick). An obviously fake, over-the-top AI clip of you doing an impossible scooter stunt between two half-built towers. Hard cut at about 1.5 seconds onto real you, same direction, clipping a kerb and going over. Labelled AI Fil, then Real Fil. It's the only one where the open is the same kind of thing as the fall, which is what the 10.4M reel did. No borrowed footage, only your face, and it can't be mistaken for anything real.

2. Re-film a famous one. One of the boys re-does a well-known viral moment for the first second or two, and we cut on the same action into your fall. This is the clothing brand's way of doing it.

3. Aftermath. Your fall, shot from a distance like a bystander's phone clip, cut before you land. Then you, sitting on the kerb covered in grass while one of the boys laughs off camera. One short line. About 8 seconds.

The other twelve are in the long version. The borrowed-clip ones are there if you want to carry the takedown risk. The calm openers (a slab being finished, a magpie, a pie) all lose because they don't open on action.

Before anyone films it

  • What it asks for. There's no product page yet, and the first 100 go out in early November through private one-to-one messages. So this can't say "pre-sale is open" to the public. It's either a teaser now, or the public launch after the first 100 sell.
  • Where. Not a footpath kerb. That's council land, and council land is banned for filming after the Old Fella. A driveway kerb on private property or a mate's place works.
  • The fall. Walking pace onto grass, pads under the clothes, a kick scooter. My rec: other shoes in the fall, because a fall in our boots can read as the boots' fault. Your call on the poll.
  • The line after. Short, and true for all four of us. The draft lines all leaned on who's paying for FAR, and that has to be exactly right before it's said on camera. We write the line at the edit brief, through the checks.
  • Check what we've already got. The Burnout grass dive and the Site File fall might already be the footage.

The "dopamine ladder"

It's a creator-course framework (Kane Kallaway's), repackaged by that account as a giveaway to get comments. The brain science underneath is real, but the slides get it wrong, the "unfinished thought is an itch" slide is an idea that's already been disproven, and the 2-second and 5-second numbers have no source.

Most of it is already in our carousel rules under other names: the hook, the open loop, holding the answer back, paying it off near the end. The one useful new idea is a check on each post: what in this one makes our people feel like they belong? A crew word, a shared enemy, replying to comments like you know them. I'll add that as a rule of thumb, not a law.
