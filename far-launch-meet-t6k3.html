Launch week: what's left to decide

Pre-read for the follow-up meeting. Saturday locked most of it. Four calls left, and this time the numbers are on the page so nobody's doing math out loud on a dodgy connection.

Already locked on Saturday. Not up for debate.

  • $20 fully refundable deposit, open to everyone who wants one. Not capped.
  • Air-freighting 100 to 150 pairs so we have boots in the country early. The rest comes by sea.
  • No Black Friday. We aim at the Christmas and Boxing Day window.
  • No paid ads until deposits prove people actually want it.
  • Afterpay on the pre-sale if we qualify.
  • No warehouse company for the first 500. We store them ourselves.
  • Posting 4 days a week: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri. Bulk filming roughly once a month. Scripts locked before filming day.
  • Jobs: Fil and Lionel edit. Grant and Con bring the ideas. Lionel runs the email side once trained. Fil does covers.
  • Everyone messages 20 to 50 of their own people when the page goes live. Grant brings Ace in.

Call 1: the price. Does everyone get $219, or just the first 100?

Grant's point from Saturday: "whether it's 219 or 240 you're still selling." Fair. So we priced all three versions properly. The boot costs us about $82 a pair landed all-in per the live budget, so the question is only about the last $10 per pair.

Locked ladderFirst 100 at $219, next 400 at $229. Full sell-through leaves about $61,500 gross before running costs.
Everyone $219Same boots, about $57,800 gross. Costs us about $4,000 across the run, and the people getting the discount are the last 400 buyers. They buy AFTER 100 sold, meaning they needed the proof, not the tenner.
Deposit locks $219Anyone who put $20 down gets the $219 price no matter when the doors open. At the deposit numbers we honestly expect, this costs a few hundred dollars total. Almost free.
Recommendation
Keep the ladder. Give the $219 lock to deposit-holders. The $20 deposit stops being "first access" and becomes "your price is locked". That's a real reason to put money down, it costs us next to nothing, and the counter mechanic on the page ("87 of 100 left at $219") stays alive. Everyone-at-219 spends four grand convincing the people who least need convincing.

Call 2: the freebies. First 100 or everyone?

Saturday leaned socks + slides for the first 100, bags for everyone, and Grant argued give it all to everyone. Two facts from the budget change this argument:

  • Socks and bags are ALREADY costed into every single order. The budget has always carried them for all 500. So "socks for the first 100 only" would actually be a cut from the current plan, not a bonus.
  • CORRECTED 27 Aug: the slides are NOT bought. Only the samples are. The budget models a 500-pair buy because the supplier's minimum is 500 to 1000 and we cannot buy 100 — but that is a plan, not a purchase, and the quote behind it ran out around 2 August. So this is a real cash decision of roughly $3,300 minimum, on a price we need to get again. It is not a question of foregone sales. Re-quote before we decide.
Recommendation
Bags and socks in every box (already the plan, already paid for). Slides stay the first-100 sweetener so "first 100" still means something, and the spares get sold online like we planned. If the room wants Grant's version, the honest cost is the lost slide sales, not new money. Decide it once, tonight.

Call 3: pick the week

The pre-sale opens with a Monday episode. Constraints already locked: filming happens at this week's session, content needs the edit gates, and the email goes to the list 12 to 24 hours before anyone else. So the real choice is WHICH Monday, and it's mostly a question of when the filming and the product page are actually ready.

Recommendation
The first Monday after the launch content is filmed, edited and gated, and the page is live and tested end to end with a real $20 test order. A launch that slips one week is forgotten in a month. A launch where the checkout breaks on night one is not.

Call 4: who fronts the launch episode

The episode that opens the queue. Options: one founder solo, or a two-hander. Never got decided on Saturday.

Recommendation
Two founders on screen. Opening the queue is a company moment, and two blokes reads like a company, not a fella with a link. Pick the pair by who's on the filming day.
