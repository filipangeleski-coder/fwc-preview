Katana, the new Higgsfield thing

What it is. A new Higgsfield mode that runs inside Claude (you type /katana). You give it a reference edit, like a trending hype edit off TikTok, or your own footage, and it rebuilds the edit around your subject. New scenes come from Higgsfield's newest video model, and it adds its own text and music.

Where it could fit us. One stylised boot hype edit built from the real studio photos. That's the same lane as the hero product film you passed: obviously produced, never posed as proof.

The catches, all from Higgsfield's own instructions for it:

  • It's built to spend without asking. Its instructions tell Claude not to ask for your OK, including on credits. Our rule is one take per shot and a check with you past about 50 credits, so any Katana brief carries a hard credit ceiling.
  • Cost. Their own test came to about 8 credits per finished second (83 credits for a 10.5 second edit). An earlier run of theirs threw away 91% of the footage and cost 120 to 135 credits per finished second. A 15 second boot edit is roughly 120 credits on a good run, which is everything left on the account.
  • It writes its own on-screen text unless told not to. Our text goes through the checks, so the brief says no text and ours goes on in CapCut.
  • With no reference given, it goes and finds a trending TikTok or Shorts edit to copy the style of. We'd pick the reference ourselves.
  • Faces. It generates people and works from whatever faces it's given. Only your face is cleared for Higgsfield. Grant, Lionel and Con stay out.
  • It's heavy on the Claude side too. Their own edit took 5.56 million agent tokens and about 100 minutes.
My read. It's a video remix tool, so it doesn't help carousels. It's worth one test for a boot hype edit when there's a slot for one and the credits to cover it (a top-up first). It's no good for the skits, because it would swap the boys for generated people, and it never goes near proof.
