The video editor

How to get a good cut first go. It lives on the dashboard under Studio, then Video.

What it is

  • The dashboard is the front counter. You hand in the footage, say what you want, watch each version and say what to change.
  • Fil's laptop is the workshop. A Claude editor cuts it with FAR's own kit and rules. A second Claude checks every version before you see it.
  • The laptop has to be on. If it's off, your job waits in line and starts when it's back.
  • Finished cuts land in the posting folder on the Drive with the cover, captions and posting copy, named after the video.

Four doors

🎥 Watch a clipYou want ideas from a clip: hooks, a cover, a caption. Nothing gets cut.
✂️ Edit itRaw footage in, a cut reel out. You steer it with notes until it's right.
🧽 Fix and packYour video is fine as filmed. It cleans the sound, fixes flicker or shake if it needs it, adds captions and a cover, and saves it ready to post.
✅ Already editedYou cut it yourself. It only makes the cover and the posting copy.

How to ask for a good cut

The editor is only as good as what you tell it. Three things make the biggest difference:

  1. The best bit. The one moment that has to be in it.
  2. The feel. Funny, proud, proof it works, or a story.
  3. What to change, in plain words, with a time if you can. "Cut the restart at 0:12."

Then tap the What can I ask? buttons. Each one writes a sentence the editor knows into your note. Change the words if you want.

What you can ask for

Tap a group to open it. The clips are from our own cuts.

Sound (4)
  • Clean the voice
    Clean up the voice and even out the volume.
  • Take the echo out
    My voice sounds roomy and echoey. Take the echo out.
  • Beep out words as a joke
    Beep out the words I name with one identical beep, and put a black bar where each is written.
  • One sound on the big beat
    Put one sound effect on the big beat only, nothing on every cut.
Picture (8)
  • Fix the flickering lights
    Fix the flickering lights.
  • Steady the shaky footage
    Steady the shaky handheld footage.
  • Pull the brightness down
    The picture is too bright. Pull the exposure down.
  • Ease the contrast back
    The contrast is too strong. Ease it back, keep the colour.
  • Keep the footage's colour
    Keep the footage's own colour, no heavy grade.
  • Blur plates and brand marks
    Blur number plates and any other brand's logo or lettering, frame by frame.
  • Keep my logos, blur plates
    Keep the logos on my cap and shirt. Blur number plates only.
  • Make it look new
    Make it look different from our last cuts. No receipt, stamp or the same grade.
Cut (12)
  • Cut the air between words
    Cut the air between my words. Keep a pause only where a line lands.
  • Cut the restart
    Cut my restart and keep only the clean take of that line.
  • Open on the action
    Open on the strongest moment from frame 1, with my voice in by about 1 second.
  • Second hook before 0:10
    Add a second hook before 0:10 so people stay past the first line.
  • Hold after the punchline
    Let the punchline land. Hold a beat after it before the next thing.
  • Right length, no padding
    Make it as long as the footage honestly holds. No padding.
  • Don't repeat a line
    Never say a line twice to fill time. Keep it shorter if it has to be.
  • Keep every line
    Keep every line and the order. Only tighten the gaps between them.
  • Start from v1's timing
    Start from the last version's timing. Change only what I say below.
  • Hard cut, no whoosh
    Hard cut straight to the next clip. No whoosh, no transition.
  • End on a finished take
    End on a finished take with a short hold after the last word. Never a restart.
  • B-roll over the flat parts
    Put b-roll from my own clips over the flat talking parts.
Words (8)
  • Caption every line
    Caption every spoken line, big and centred, one line at a time.
  • Highlight the key word
    Highlight the key word in each caption with the white chip.
  • Hook line on frame 1
    Put a short hook line on frame 1 and hold it alone for 1.5 seconds.
  • Star the swears in text
    Star the swears in any text. Leave them spoken in the audio.
  • No text at all
    No text on screen at all. Let the footage carry it.
  • Role plate, never a name
    Add a lower-third role plate for who is talking, like THE FORMWORKER. Never a name.
  • Cover: two to four words
    Cover label: the setup in two to four words, never the payoff.
  • Label the thing on the word
    Draw a line to the thing I'm talking about and label it, once, on the word.
Moves (8)
  • Punch in, not on the joke
    Punch in on the setup words and keep the punchline itself clean.
  • Replay the big moment slow
    Play the big moment in full, then rewind and replay it at half speed with a freeze and a label.
  • Freeze on the hero frame
    Slow it under the word, then freeze on the hero frame so the line can land.
  • Whip between scenes
    Whip between the two scenes with no black in between.
  • Shake on the hit
    Shake the frame once on the hit, nothing else.
  • Big type behind me
    Put the big word behind me on the line, so it sits between me and the background.
  • Quiet doco look
    Make it a quiet documentary: black bars, role plates, chapter cards, no hook.
  • One paper graphic
    Use one paper graphic, a receipt or slip, only on a spoken price or list.

Changing a version

  • Watch it, then write what to change or tap the buttons.
  • Tick Just fix it if you only want those changes and nothing else touched.
  • Tap Make v2. You get a ping when it's ready.
  • Three rounds of changes per video.
  • Happy with one? Pick the day and tap Use this one. The final files land in that day's folder.
  • A Fix and pack you don't like? Tap Not right? Edit it properly.

What it can't do yet

  • Songs. Coming next. For now, add the song in the Instagram app when you post.
  • A timeline to drag clips around. Not in the dashboard. Open the cut in CapCut for that.
