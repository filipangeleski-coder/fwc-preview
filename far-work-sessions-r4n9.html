Work sessions

Now on for all four of us. Two minutes to read, then try one on your phone.

The short version
Chat answers a question. A Work session does a job: it reads FAR's files and the dashboard, then writes the thing for you, step by step, while you watch.

What it is

Claude, running on FAR's own server, with its own copy of every FAR file. Each message you send also hands it a fresh look at the dashboard: the tasks, the filming days, the polls and the boss's jobs.

So you can say "write the shot list for Sunday from the Ideas board" or "turn this card into an email to Don" and it goes and does it, file by file.

How to start one

  • In Chat, flip the switch at the bottom from Chat to Work session.
  • Or tap 🛠 Work session on a card, a task, a boss job or an email. That way it already knows what you are working on.
  • On a Chat answer, tap Use in work session to carry it across.

What it can't do

  • It can't send anything. No emails, no posts, no messages, no payments. It writes; one of us sends.
  • It can't search the web. Research stays in Chat. When it needs something looked up, it gives you a "Look this up in Chat" line with a button.
  • Its changes stay in your own copy. Nothing it edits changes the files the rest of us see, yet. Copy out what you want to keep.

The limits

These are what the work would cost at Claude's list price. Nobody gets a bill. They come out of FAR's shared Claude allowance, so the limits stop one busy day eating everyone's week.

Two of us can have a message running at once. Your own second message waits until your first one finishes.

Good to know

  • The other three can see what you type in a Work session. Keep anything private out of it.
  • It closes itself after an hour of quiet. Send a new message and it picks up where you left off.
  • A long job can take a few minutes. A working pill shows while it runs, and the back button gets you out without stopping it.
Try this first
Open any task on your list, tap 🛠 Work session, and ask: "What do I need to do to finish this, and can you draft the first step?"
