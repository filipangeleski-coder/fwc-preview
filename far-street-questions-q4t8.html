Street questions

FAR · Tue 20 Oct 2026 · an idea to try when the merch lands, before the boots do

Tell us what you reckon by Saturday: two quick questions on the dashboard.

What it is

Two of us walk up to a tradie, ask one question about something he's seen on the job, and film his answer on a phone. One asks, one films and says nothing. Hold his reaction, ask one follow-up at most, say thanks.

Then ask if we can use it (first name and trade, no tag), and hand him a FAR thank-you: a stubby holder or a sticker, a cap or tee for the best ones. Everyone who stops gets one, whether we use the clip or not. The clip runs 15 to 30 seconds and his voice carries it.

Why now

The first 100 pay from early November, but the boots finish on 28 Nov and the rest arrive by sea in January. That's a long wait with nothing to hand anyone.

Merch lands first. Caps and tees leave the factory by 5 Nov, so they should arrive around mid November (not confirmed). Stubby holders, stickers and fresheners are paid for and in production.

Our best question posts ask a tradie what he's seen. This is the same thing, face to face, with real tradies who aren't us.

Questions to pick from

One question per outing. Questions about what you've seen land better than questions about yourself.

  1. Biggest stuff-up you've ever seen on a job? (Then: what happened next?)
  2. What's the worst nickname on your crew? (Then: what did he do to earn it?)
  3. What's the best thing you've ever found inside a wall?
  4. What's the worst 'she'll be right' you've seen actually go wrong?
  5. What's the most ridiculous thing a homeowner's asked for halfway through a build?
  6. What's the biggest dummy spit you've seen on site?
  7. What's the most someone's charged for a job that took ten minutes?
  8. What's the funniest thing you've seen a machine operator hit?

How to do it

  • Two of us: one asks, one films on a phone. Nobody's assigned; whoever's keen picks a day.
  • Best spot to start: outside a cafe, trade supplier or yard, after the owner or manager says yes. That's private property, so no council form.
  • Early morning or knock-off, when blokes have a minute.
  • Get the yes on camera first ('All good if this goes on our Instagram?'), then a quick texted release after. A screenshot of his yes is enough to keep.
  • One question, one follow-up, thanks, then the thank-you item. Never a prize, never 'win'.

The rules

  • Never on a live construction site, and never in a way that looks like council workers on the job (the Old Fella lesson).
  • A public footpath counts as council land. Sydney (City of Sydney): a small handheld shoot needs no form. Wollongong: commercial filming on council land needs an application lodged 5 business days ahead. Our own rule says no council land, so start with owner's-OK spots.
  • No under-18s without a parent's signature.
  • If his shirt or hi-vis shows his employer's logo, blur it or don't use the clip. Never name his employer.
  • Don't cut his words to say something he didn't mean, and don't caption him as if he backs the boots. He's answering a question, not endorsing FAR.
  • No ask in the clip itself. It's a question post, like Smoko.
