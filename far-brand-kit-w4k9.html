The FAR brand kit

Everything that never changes. Colours, the logo, type, and the five rules that apply every time a camera comes out. Grant, Lionel — read it, then tell us what is wrong with it. Your notes are the point of this page.

Why it all looks like this

Blundstone is white and blue. Steel Blue is white and blue. Redback is white and red. CAT is yellow. BRUNT is orange. Every accent colour in this category is already owned by someone bigger than us.

So FAR does not play. Pure dark monochrome, no accent colour, ever. The content carries the drama and the brand gets out of the way. That is the whole idea, and it is why a lot of the rules below sound strict.

The logo. Three marks, not one.

The wordmark
The main one. Shopify header, box, bag tag, QR card, end frame of a founder video.
The bare A
Instagram profile pic, sock band, anywhere too small for the full word.
Third mark
In the set, role not locked. Fil to confirm before anyone uses it.

Look at the A. It is not a typed A. It is drawn, with a notch cut through it like a blade. That notch is the brand. If you ever see FAR just typed out in a normal font, that is wrong and it is not our logo.

Goes on: Shopify header, box exterior, boot bag tag, QR card, Instagram profile picture, end frame of a founder video.

Never goes on: destruction tests (a logo makes proof look like an ad), on-site footage overlays, stat graphics, anything white or light, busy backgrounds, or anywhere near a competitor logo.

Never stretch it, never recolour it, never put a shadow on it. Minimum 32px on a screen, 15mm in print. When in doubt, leave it off. The content speaks.

Black is the only background, always. White carries every headline and button. Smoke is for small supporting text only. No fourth colour. Not one.

Type. Two fonts, and they do not swap jobs.

Space Grotesk BoldEvery headline, every CapCut overlay, cover text, big numbers.
DM Sans RegularBody copy, captions, email text, anything small and supporting.

Both are free on Google Fonts and both are in Canva — search the exact names. Never mix the roles. Headlines are never DM Sans. Body is never Space Grotesk. No exceptions.

The five rules. These are the filming-day ones.

1. Frame 1 is always physicalThe first frame shows something happening to a body or a boot. Never a product sitting still, never text, never a talking head.
2. Dark or dirty backgrounds onlyConcrete, gravel, mud, near-black. If the background is clean or bright, reshoot. The site is the studio.
3. One thing in focusClose on the insole, the heel, the feet. Tight. Wide shots are context, never the hero frame.
4. No graphics over real footageNo borders, shapes or decorations on site footage. Text only if it adds proof, a number or a label. The footage is the brand.
5. Face the lightFace the natural light. No ring lights, no studio setups. Raw light on a face reads real. Produced light reads like an ad.
